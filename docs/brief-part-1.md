### The look, and the rules that bind this cut

The look is the cold open's, and `brief-cold-open.md` copies it from the script
in full: the evidence table as the spine, DATA and SCHEMATIC, the palette, the
two typefaces, the citation of the study under discussion with its design tag,
and "Not medical advice" on every frame. The table keeps the rows and columns
the cold open drew: H1 MEMORY, H2 ATTENTION and H3 DOPAMINE, under PREDICTION,
RESULT and STATUS. Three lines of the look matter most here:

> DATA and SCHEMATIC. A figure marked DATA is drawn from values the paper
> reports: point estimates, intervals, percentages. A figure marked SCHEMATIC
> shows a design or a direction and has no scale. Nothing is drawn that looks
> like data and is not.

> A failed prediction: red, #D2452D. The struck line and the failed box.
> Nothing else.

> Sound. A slow pulse and one sustained string line. The line moves one step at
> each status line and completes only in Part 5. A soft tick for each row of
> data.

From "The register":

> 1. Design before result. Authors, year, design, sample. Then the finding.
> 2. A number has a unit, and on screen an interval. Effect sizes are spoken as
>    numbers. Adjectives do not replace them.
> 4. Every result is followed by its strongest limitation. In the next
>    sentence, not in a disclaimer at the end.
> 5. A status line, not a verdict. Involved. Not supported. Not established.
>    Contested.

From "Lines that must not get stronger in the edit":

> "Not supported as the defining deficit" must not turn into "memory is fine".

From "Build now, later, never":

> Never. A figure that looks like data and is not. A number on screen without

## Amendments

The build is script version 3 with these amendments and nothing else. They
belong in the next version of the script.

### P1. Narration, sentence p06

Was:

> In 2017, Skodzik, Holling and Pedersen separated those stages in a
> meta-analysis of long-term memory in adults with ADHD.

Now:

> Skodzik, Holling and Pedersen separated those stages in a meta-analysis of
> long-term memory in adults with ADHD, published in 2017.

Why. The same case as the first sentence of the cold open (A8), and a clearer
one. PubMed has carried the paper since November 2013, and its print issue is
February 2017. "In 2017, [they] separated" dates the work to a year in which it
was more than three years old. The citation on screen keeps 2017, the year of
the issue.

### P2. Narration, sentence p12

Was:

> In 2024, a study of two hundred and five adolescents and adults with ADHD
> found initial learning preserved and delayed recall reduced.

Now:

> A study of two hundred and five adolescents and adults with ADHD, published
> in 2024, found initial learning preserved and delayed recall reduced.

Why. The same form as A8 and P1. The data are the baseline assessment of an
earlier trial. 2024 is the year of publication.

### P3. Narration, sentence p25

Was:

> On the second, sixteen randomised trials of cognitive training.

Now:

> On the second, fifteen randomised trials of cognitive training.

Why. The paper's abstract says "Sixteen of 695 nonduplicate records were
analyzed". Its Results say "Fifteen trials (reported in 16 papers) met entry
criteria": two of the sixteen papers report one trial. The 759 children add up
only when that trial is counted once. The design tag changes with the sentence,
from 16 RCTS to `META-ANALYSIS, 15 RCTS, N = 759`.

### P4. Two citations are completed

| Study | Script | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heritability | Faraone and Larsson (2019). | `Faraone and Larsson (2019). Mol Psychiatry 24(4), 562–575.` Tag: `REVIEW, 37 TWIN STUDIES` |
| Working memory in children | Martinussen et al. (2005). | `Martinussen et al. (2005). J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry 44(4), 377–384.` |

Why. Every other citation of the part carries journal, volume and pages, and
the script lists "the reference for the heritability figure" among the details
to confirm. Both references are confirmed. The script gives the heritability
figure no design tag. The paper is a review, and the figure is its mean over 37
twin studies.

The other five citations and tags stand as the script has them:
`Skodzik, Holling and Pedersen (2017). J Atten Disord 21(4), 267–283.` with
`META-ANALYSIS, ADULTS`. `Pawley et al. (2024). Neuroscience Applied 3, 103941.`
with `CASE-CONTROL, 205 VS 50`. `Alderson et al. (2013). Neuropsychology 27(3), 287–302.`
with `META-ANALYSIS, 38 STUDIES, ADULTS`. `Willcutt et al. (2005). Biol Psychiatry 57(11), 1336–1346.`
with `META-ANALYSIS, 83 STUDIES, N = 3,734 WITH ADHD`.
`Cortese et al. (2015). J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry 54(3), 164–174.`
The tag for Martinussen stands too: `META-ANALYSIS, 26 STUDIES, CHILDREN`.

### P5. The heritability line says what the figure is

Under `HERITABILITY NEAR 0.74, TWIN STUDIES` stands one note:

> Mean heritability across 37 twin studies of ADHD or of inattention and hyperactivity: 74%

Why. The script's rule is "A number has a unit, and on screen an interval." The
paper gives no interval for the 74 percent. It gives what the number is a mean
of, and that is shown in its place.

### P6. The overlap figure says that it is computed

The script marks the figure "DATA, computed". A DATA mark means "drawn from
values the paper reports", and this figure is drawn from no paper. So the word
`COMPUTED` stands beside the mark, and a note states what was computed:

> Two normal curves of equal spread, half a standard deviation apart. Shared area: 80.3%

The script's on-screen line is set in two parts, `d = 0.5` and
`DISTRIBUTIONS OVERLAP BY 80%`. The axis is named
`SCORE, IN STANDARD DEVIATIONS`, and its two ticks are the two means, 0 and
0.5. The two curves are named `COMPARISON` and `ADHD`, in the colours the
palette gives them. The marked case is named `A SINGLE CASE`.

### P7. The visual finding is given at the strength the paper gives it

| Script | Now |
| --- | --- |
| VISUAL: NO DIFFERENCE | `VISUAL` · `NO SIGNIFICANT DIFFERENCE` |

Why. The same case as A6 in the cold open. The paper's pooled estimate for
visual long-term memory is −0.36, a third of a standard deviation in the same
direction as the verbal one, from ten effect sizes, and its interval runs from
−0.94 to 0.22. That is a difference that was not significant. "No difference"
is stronger than that. The other two findings stand as the script has them:
`VERBAL LONG-TERM MEMORY` · `WEAKER` and `RETRIEVAL` · `NO DEFICIT OBSERVED`.
The narration follows in P14.

### P8. The findings of both studies carry their statistics

| Study | Finding | Value | Note under it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2017 | `VERBAL LONG-TERM MEMORY` | `WEAKER` | d = −0.49, 99% CI [−0.71, −0.27] |
| 2017 | `VISUAL` | `NO SIGNIFICANT DIFFERENCE` | d = −0.36, 99% CI [−0.94, 0.22] |
| 2024 | `INITIAL LEARNING` | `PRESERVED` | Standardised difference −0.22, 95% CI [−0.55, 0.10], p = .175 |
| 2024 | `DELAYED RECALL` | `REDUCED` | Standardised difference −0.38, 95% CI [−0.70, −0.06], p = .021 |

Why. "A number has a unit, and on screen an interval." With the estimates on
screen, a viewer sees what each word rests on. WEAKER rests on an interval
that excludes zero, and NO SIGNIFICANT DIFFERENCE on an estimate of −0.36 whose
interval is wide. PRESERVED is the authors' word, and it rests on a difference
that was not significant and whose interval reaches −0.55.

The meta-analysis draws 99 percent intervals in its forest plots, and the
screen says so. The 2024 study reports 95 percent intervals.

### P9. The four estimates for children carry their intervals

| Row | Estimate | Interval |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SPATIAL STORAGE` | 0.85 | [0.62, 1.08] |
| `SPATIAL CENTRAL EXECUTIVE` | 1.06 | [0.72, 1.39] |
| `VERBAL STORAGE` | 0.47 | [0.36, 0.59] |
| `VERBAL CENTRAL EXECUTIVE` | 0.43 | [0.24, 0.62] |

Why. The script draws this plot from point estimates and asks for the intervals

forest row without its interval is not a forest row, and the rule is "on screen

### P10. The two symptom rows are named as the paper names its raters

| Script | Now |
| --- | --- |
| SYMPTOMS, UNBLINDED | `SYMPTOMS, MOST PROXIMAL RATER` |
| SYMPTOMS, PROBABLY BLINDED | `SYMPTOMS, PROBABLY BLINDED RATER` |

Why. The paper's first analysis takes the rater "most proximal to the treatment
setting", whom it calls "typically unblinded". In several trials that rater was
also the probably blinded one. UNBLINDED states as a fact what the paper states
as a tendency. The four estimates and intervals stand as the script has them:
`VERBAL WORKING MEMORY` 0.52 [0.24, 0.80], `VISUAL WORKING MEMORY` 0.47
[0.23, 0.70], 0.37 [0.09, 0.66] and 0.20 [0.01, 0.40].

### P11. A note under the training plot: training of working memory alone

> Training of working memory alone, 6 trials: ADHD symptoms 0.00 [−0.31, 0.31],
> rated by the most proximal rater.

Why. The second test is "reducing it should reduce the disorder", and "it" is
the working-memory deficit. The four rows pool every kind of cognitive
training. The paper also reports the six trials that trained working memory
alone: working memory improved, and symptoms did not move, even for the rater
most likely to see an effect. That is the paper's own answer to the test, and
its abstract says so: "There were no effects of working memory training,
specifically on ADHD symptoms."

Without this note, the second box is marked as failed beside a row whose
interval excludes zero, 0.20 [0.01, 0.40]. The note arrives after the sentence
that calls the test "not decisive", and before the box is marked.

### P12. The adult estimates are given in a word, and are not drawn

Under `ADULTS · ALDERSON ET AL. 2013` stand two rows, `PHONOLOGICAL` and
`VISUOSPATIAL`, each with the value `MODERATE` and no mark on the axis.

Why. The script: "The film speaks no number here, so draw none without the
paper." The paper's full text could not be read. Its abstract reports
"moderate-magnitude between-group effect sizes" in both domains, and names the
two domains phonological and visuospatial.

### P14. Narration, sentence p08

Was:

> Visual memory did not.

Now:

> Visual memory showed no significant difference.

Why. The voice says what the screen says (P7), as in the cold open (A6). "Did

object to the flat no in a seminar.

## Decisions of the build

- **The citation band names one study at a time, and leaves when the film
  speaks for itself.** It is away under the computed curves, the prediction,
  the two check boxes and the status line, as it is under the word DEFICIT in
  the cold open (B2).
- **The first cell of row H1 fills on the words that state the prediction,**
  "in retention and in retrieval", at the end of the sentence. The script lists
  the cell before the three stages. The stages draw on "where memories are kept
  and recovered", so that each mark lands on its own words.
- **The table is drawn to be filled.** It spans the live width, and a row holds
  three lines of a cell. The cold open's table is narrower and holds no text.
  When the parts are joined, the cold open's table should take this geometry.
- **No camera move,** as in the cold open (B1).
- **The beats start where the script starts them.** The narration is paced at
  137 written words a minute, the script's pace, which leaves between one third
  of a second and three seconds of hold at the end of each beat.
