### 0:00 · Cold open: an untested assumption

Loops. Opens L01 (why did learning not follow?). Opens L00 (deficit of what?).

Narration, word for word:

> In 2022, a triple-masked crossover trial tested an assumption that had rarely
> been tested directly. One hundred and seventy-three children with ADHD
> received extended-release methylphenidate for three weeks and placebo for
> three weeks, in randomised order, while being taught academic content in
> small groups.
>
> Under methylphenidate, arithmetic output rose by thirty-seven percent per
> minute, and rule violations fell by fifty-three percent per hour.
>
> The amount of content the children learned did not differ between conditions.
>
> *(pause, 2 seconds)*
>
> The drug did what it is known to do. Learning did not follow.
>
> The expectation that it would follow rests on a model carried in the name of
> the diagnosis: a deficit. Three candidates recur, in clinical explanation and
> in public belief. A deficit of memory. A deficit of attention. A deficit of
> dopamine. Each makes predictions that can be checked.
>
> This film checks them.

Picture. Black. The citation line first, alone. Then the trial design draws
itself as a crossover diagram (SCHEMATIC): two parallel tracks, sequence AB and
sequence BA, each with two three-week blocks. The blocks are hatched, because
the allocation is masked, and the hatching clears to show which block is
methylphenidate and which is placebo. Under the diagram, two result rows grow
from a zero line (DATA): plus 37 percent, minus 53 percent. A third row appears
with no bar at all, only its label. Hold on the three rows in silence. Then the
diagram recedes, the word DEFICIT is set large, and an empty table draws under
it: three rows, three columns. It stays empty.

Sound. Almost nothing. A low room tone. One soft tick as each row lands. Two
seconds of silence after the third row. A single sustained low note under the
last sentence.

### The look

The idea in one line. A paper that moves. Every frame is a figure, a table or a
citation, drawn in time with the narration.

The spine is the evidence table. Three rows: memory, attention, dopamine. Three
columns: prediction, result, status. It is empty at the end of the cold open
and complete at the end of Part 3. It is the film's progress bar and its
summary.

DATA and SCHEMATIC. A figure marked DATA is drawn from values the paper
reports: point estimates, intervals, percentages. A figure marked SCHEMATIC
shows a design or a direction and has no scale. Nothing is drawn that looks
like data and is not.

The palette.

| Ground | near black | `#0E0F12` |
| Type and axes | bone | `#E9E4D8` |
| Grid and secondary lines | graphite | `#5B6068` |

| Conjecture: deep blue ground | | `#0F2A4A` |

Type. One condensed sans for tags and table heads. One text sans with tabular
figures for numbers and citations. Minus signs are real minus signs, and
intervals stand in square brackets.

Always on screen. The citation of the study under discussion, its design tag,
and the words "Not medical advice".

Camera. A slow push-in on a figure while it draws. A hold on every result. Cuts
and slow dissolves, and no other transition.

Sound. A slow pulse and one sustained string line. The line moves one step at
each status line and completes only in Part 5. A soft tick for each row of
data.

What the film never shows. Faces, children, classrooms, glowing brains, stock
footage, pictures of pills.

### Rules that bind this cut

- The register, rule 7: No scenes, no characters, no second person. Nothing is
  set anywhere, and nobody is addressed.
- Cuts and slices: "The cold open with the title, 1:17. It is also the
  trailer."
- In every cut, without exception: the words "Not medical advice".
- Claims and sources, "The trial's own limits": A summer programme, three-week
  units, small-group teaching. The authors plan a replication in ordinary

- Never: a figure that looks like data and is not. A number on screen without

### Claims and sources for this beat

- The trial. Pelham et al. 2022, Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology
  90(5), 367 to 380. 173 children aged 7 to 12 with DSM-5 ADHD in a therapeutic
  summer programme. Triple-masked, within-subject AB/BA crossover. Two
  three-week phases of daily 25-minute lessons in science, social studies and
  vocabulary, in groups of 10 to 14. OROS methylphenidate against placebo.
  Effects on seatwork and behaviour on every day of instruction. No detectable
  effect on learning.
  - Paper: <https://escholarship.org/uc/item/9br9z1fj>
  - PubMed: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35604744/>
- 37 percent, 53 percent, 1.7 points. These three figures are from the
  university's summary of the trial, not from the abstract. **Confirm them in
  the paper's tables before they go on screen.**
  - Summary: <https://news.fiu.edu/2022/long-thought-to-be-the-key-to-academic-success,-medication-doesnt-help-kids-with-adhd-learn,-study-finds>
- "Rarely been tested directly." The paper names two earlier studies with a
  causal design.

## Amendments after the fact-check

The build is script version 3 with these five amendments and nothing else.
Each one answers a finding in `fact-check-cold-open.md`. They belong in the
next version of the script.

### A1. Narration, third sentence

Was:

> Under methylphenidate, arithmetic output rose by thirty-seven percent per
> minute, and rule violations fell by fifty-three percent per hour.

Now:

> Under methylphenidate, the children completed thirty-seven percent more
> arithmetic problems per minute of seatwork, and committed fifty-three percent
> fewer rule violations per hour.

Why. The old line attached the time unit to the percentage, so it could be
heard as a rate of change. It also ran "being taught academic content" straight
into "arithmetic output", as if arithmetic had been the taught content. It was
a separate seatwork session. The new line uses the paper's own verbs: "Children
completed 37% more arithmetic problems per minute", "Children committed 53%
fewer rule violations per hour".

### A2. The three result rows carry their measure and their statistic

| Row | Label | Value | Note under the row |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | ARITHMETIC PROBLEMS CORRECT PER MINUTE | +37% | Academic seatwork. Difference +1.79 per minute, 95% CI [1.68, 1.90] |
| 2 | RULE VIOLATIONS PER HOUR | −53% | Classroom. Difference −2.02 per hour, 95% CI [−2.18, −1.86] |
| 3 | CURRICULUM CONTENT LEARNED | NO DETECTABLE EFFECT | Unit tests, before and after. Test × medication: p = .73 (subject area), p = .75 (vocabulary) |

Why. The script's own rule is "A number has a unit, and on screen an
interval." The paper gives an interval only for the raw difference, so that is
the interval shown. The percentages stay, because they are the authors' stated
figures and the narration speaks them. The two means (6.7 against 5.0, and 1.9
against 3.9) are not shown: beside "+37%" they would invite a division that
gives 34%, an inconsistency that is the paper's and that the film cannot
resolve. The label of row 1 gains the word CORRECT, because the measure is
problems correctly completed. Row 3 still has no bar.

### A3. The limits line, shown because the cold open ships alone

> Limits: one summer programme, 3-week units, small groups with behaviour
> management, 80% of children on 18 mg. The authors call for replication in
> ordinary classrooms over a school year.

Why. The script lists a summer programme, three-week units and small-group
teaching, and says the authors "plan" a replication. The paper's first listed
limitation is dose, which the script omits. Its second is small-group teaching
"with behavior management procedures in place". The paper calls for a
replication. It does not announce one.

### A4. The crossover diagram leaves a gap between the two blocks

Why. Five days separated the two phases. The diagram is a schematic and has no
scale, so the gap carries no label. Each block is labelled "3 WEEKS", which is
the abstract's wording for a phase of 12 teaching days.

### A5. Two source notes corrected

- "in groups of 10 to 14" was the class size. The teaching groups were 1 to 11
  children.
- "daily 25-minute lessons in science, social studies and vocabulary": each
  afternoon had two 25-minute lessons, one on subject-area content and one on
  vocabulary. Each child had social studies in one phase and science in the
  other.

Neither note reaches the screen.
