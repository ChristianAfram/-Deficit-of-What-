The writer's flag is cleared in one sense: 37 percent and 53 percent are in the

running text of the Results. No table contains them, so "confirm them in the
paper's tables" cannot be met literally.

## Decisions, and how each was settled

The writer agreed on 15 November 2026 to settle these in favour of the frame a
specialist cannot fault. Each decision below ends with what was done. The
resulting amendments to the script are listed in `brief-cold-open.md` (A1 to
A5).

### 1. The two percentages do not recompute from the numbers printed beside them

| | Paper states | From the printed means | From the printed difference | Interval of the difference, as a share of the placebo mean |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arithmetic problems per minute | 37% more | 6.7 against 5.0 is +34.0% | 1.79 over 5.0 is +35.8% | +33.6% to +38.0% |
| Rule violations per hour | 53% fewer | 1.9 against 3.9 is −51.3% | 2.02 over 3.9 is −51.8% | −47.7% to −55.9% |

The paper does not say how it derived 37 and 53. A viewer who looks up the
paper and divides gets 34 and 51.

The film's own rule is "A number has a unit, and on screen an interval." The
paper gives an interval only for the raw difference (supplement, Table S1):

- Arithmetic: 6.7 against 5.0 problems per minute. Difference +1.79, 95% CI
  [1.68, 1.90].
- Rule violations: 1.9 against 3.9 per hour. Difference −2.02, 95% CI
  [−2.18, −1.86].

**Settled (A2).** The percentages stay, spoken and on screen, because they are
the authors' stated figures. Each row gains a note with the raw difference and
its interval, which meets the interval rule with the only interval the paper
gives. The two means are not shown: beside "+37%" they would invite the
division that gives 34%, and the film cannot resolve an inconsistency that is
the paper's. I confirmed both intervals myself in Table S1 of the supplement.

Still open: upload title 2, "Methylphenidate Raised Output by 37%", carries the
same exposure. Title 1 does not.

### 2. Two narration lines attach the time unit to the percentage

"arithmetic output rose by thirty-seven percent per minute" and "rule
violations fell by fifty-three percent per hour" can be heard as rates of
change. The paper says "37% more arithmetic problems per minute" and "53% fewer
rule violations per hour".

**Settled (A1).** The sentence now uses the paper's own verbs: "the children
completed thirty-seven percent more arithmetic problems per minute of seatwork,
and committed fifty-three percent fewer rule violations per hour."

### 3. Arithmetic was not what was being taught

The narration runs "while being taught academic content in small groups"
straight into "arithmetic output rose". Arithmetic was a separate ten-minute
seatwork session each morning, which the paper uses as a manipulation check.
The taught content, in the afternoon, was science or social studies, plus
vocabulary. The measure is problems *correctly* completed per minute.

**Settled (A1, A2).** The narration now says "of seatwork". The row label reads
ARITHMETIC PROBLEMS CORRECT PER MINUTE, and its note begins "Academic
seatwork". The paper's term for the measure is "academic seatwork
productivity".

### 4. The limits line for the cold open as a film of its own

The script says to state the trial's limits if the cold open ships alone, and
lists: a summer programme, three-week units, small-group teaching. Against the
paper:

- The paper lists two limitations. The first is dose: "the mean dosage of
  OROS-MPH may be small relative to current prescribing practices". Eighty
  percent of the children took 18 mg. The script's list omits it, and
  clinicians will look for it first.
- The second is small-group teaching "with behavior management procedures in
  place".
- The paper presents the three-week unit as a strength ("ecologically valid
  timeframe"), not as a limit.
- "The authors plan a replication" is the university summary. The paper says a
  replication in ordinary classrooms over a school year "would be particularly
  valuable". It calls for one. It does not announce one.

**Settled (A3).** The limits line names the summer programme, the three-week
units, small groups with behaviour management, and the dose (80% of children on
18 mg). It says the authors call for a replication, not that they plan one.

### 5. "Three weeks" is the abstract's shorthand

Each phase was 12 teaching weekdays, 13 measured days, with a five-day break
between the two phases. "Three weeks" is the paper's own wording and is
defensible.

**Settled (A4).** The narration keeps "three weeks". The crossover diagram is a
schematic and has no scale, so the break is drawn as a plain gap between the
blocks, without a label.

### 6. Beat 3.6, outside this build: 1.7 points is the non-significant estimate

"Medication taken on the day of a test raised scores by 1.7 points in a
hundred" uses the subject-area estimate. It was not significant: difference
0.20 of 12 items, 95% CI [−0.07, 0.46], p = .15, d = 0.09 [−0.03, 0.20]. The
significant effect is vocabulary: 3.0 points, 0.59 of 20 items [0.17, 1.02],
p = .007, d = 0.19 [0.05, 0.32]. Test-day dosing was also not manipulated on
its own. The figure is the main effect of the medicated phase, averaged over
pre-test and post-test. "Performance moved" rests on the 1.7 as written.

**To settle when Part 3 is built.** The defensible form shows both: vocabulary
+3.0 points (p = .007) and subject area +1.7 points (p = .15, not
significant). It is outside this build, so nothing was changed.

### 7. Two notes in "Claims and sources" are wrong against the paper

- "in groups of 10 to 14": that is the class size. The teaching groups were 1
  to 11 children, with means of 6.2 and 5.1. The figure comes from the
  university summary.
- "daily 25-minute lessons in science, social studies and vocabulary": each
  afternoon had two 25-minute lessons, one on subject-area content and one on
  vocabulary. Each child had social studies in one phase and science in the
  other.

**Settled (A5).** Both notes are corrected in the brief. Neither reaches the
screen in this build.

## What the paper reports, for reference

- **Arithmetic** (problems correctly completed per minute, ten minutes of
  seatwork each morning, multilevel model over 13 days per phase): marginal
  means 6.7 against 5.0. Difference 1.79 [1.68, 1.90]. F(1, 7656.2) = 1007.39,
  p < .001. No standardised effect size.
- **Rule violations per hour**: marginal means 1.9 against 3.9. Difference
  −2.02 [−2.18, −1.86]. F(1, 7017.8) = 610.72, p < .001. The day-by-medication
  interaction was p = .03. No standardised effect size.
- **Learning**: test-by-medication interaction, subject-area F(1, 514.0) =
  0.12, p = .73. Vocabulary F = 0.10, p = .75. The paper gives no estimate,
  interval, Bayes factor or equivalence test for the interaction. Raw gains:
  subject-area +1.9 on placebo against +1.8 on methylphenidate, of 12 items.
  Vocabulary +6.3 against +6.4, of 20 items. The only intervals are in the
  supplement's check on phase 1 alone: d = 0.11 [−0.13, 0.35] and d = 0.08
  [−0.28, 0.43]. They do not exclude a small benefit, which is why "no
  detectable effect" is the right strength and "no effect" would not be.
- **Design details**: data collected in the summers of 2014 to 2016. The
  learning study was a sub-study inside a larger trial of tolerance to
  methylphenidate. 239 enrolled in that trial, 66 excluded before the
  sub-study, 173 randomised. Two randomisations: drug order by child,
  curriculum order by small group. Doses: 80% on 18 mg, 16% on 27 mg, 4% on
  36 mg, mean 0.64 mg/kg a day.
- **Earlier randomised studies the paper names**: Molina et al. (2009) and
  Tamm et al. (2017). The paper calls itself "the first study to evaluate
  stimulant effects on the learning of standard units of academic material in a
  controlled classroom setting", so "rarely been tested directly" is safe.
